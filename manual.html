Section 1

Getting Started, Reviewer Tag & Saving

Before You Begin: Complete Your Literature Screening First

Meta-Ethnography Canvas is designed specifically for the post-screening synthesis phases (eMERGe Phases 1 and 3–7; the Phase 1 aim is entered in the startup setup window). Literature database searching, deduplication, title/abstract screening, and PRISMA flow diagram tracking are outside the scope of this tool.

Please complete your systematic search and study selection in your preferred reference manager or screening tool (such as Zotero, EndNote, Rayyan, or Covidence) first, and then import the final set of included full-text PDFs here for deep reading, appraisal, and conceptual translation.

System Requirements (Desktop & Online)
  • Desktop or Laptop Computer: Because meta-ethnography involves reading full-text PDFs side-by-side and dragging concepts across wide synthesis matrices, the workspace is designed for desktop/laptop screens with a mouse or trackpad (smartphones and small tablets are not supported).
  • Internet Connection: While your PDFs and synthesis data stay 100% private in your browser, an active internet connection is required to load the PDF rendering engine and Word export libraries.
  • Recommended Browsers: Up-to-date versions of Google Chrome, Microsoft Edge, Firefox, or Safari.

1.1 Project Code & Reviewer Name

When you open the app for the first time, a setup window will ask for your Reviewer Name or Initials (e.g., DYJ) and a Project Code (e.g., PALLIATIVE-2026).

  • Reviewer Name: Every quote you extract and theme you create will automatically carry your tag (such as @DYJ). You can change it anytime in the top bar.
  • Project Code: This unique code prevents you from accidentally merging or overwriting backup files from a different systematic review project.
  • Phase 1 Aim: You can optionally enter your research question here; it will be displayed in your Line of Argument drawer to keep you focused during synthesis.

1.2 How Saving & Backups Work (Important)

Auto-Save: All of your work—including uploaded PDFs, highlights, matrix rows, and notes—is saved automatically inside your web browser's local database (IndexedDB). A green Saved badge appears in the top bar whenever you make a change.

Always Keep a JSON Backup

Because browser storage stays on your current computer and browser, clearing your browser data or switching computers will not carry your project over automatically.

  • Click Backup in the top bar at the end of each work session to download a lightweight .json project file.
  • Use the Settings Menu (⚙️) ➔ Restore Project to load your .json backup on any computer. To respect publisher copyright and keep the backup file lightweight, PDF files themselves are not saved inside the .json file—simply drag and drop the original PDFs (with the same filenames) back onto the screen and they will reconnect to your highlights automatically!
Section 2

Tab 1: Extract & Context

Tab 1 is your reading and extraction workspace. It is divided into three columns: your Study List on the left, the PDF Reader in the center, and a resizable Study Details & Extracted Concepts sidebar on the right.

2.1 Adding Studies, Setting the Index Paper & One-Click Sorting

  • Click + Add PDF in the left sidebar (or drag and drop PDF files anywhere on the window).
  • Index Paper (#1): The study at the top of the left sidebar is automatically marked with an orange Index badge. It appears as the first column in your Synthesis Matrix.
  • One-Click Methodological Sorting: Use the Year▲ button to sort studies chronologically by publication year, or Rich★ to sort by Conceptual Richness and Methodological Quality (putting your richest, highest-quality paper at #1 as the Index Paper). You can also drag and drop studies manually at any time. Every order change is automatically logged in your Audit Trail.

2.2 Reading PDFs, Zooming & Searching Inside PDFs

  • Zoom Controls: Use the floating dark toolbar at the top of the PDF viewer to select a zoom level from the dropdown menu (50% to 300%, default 130%) or click the −, +, and Reset buttons.
  • Full-PDF Keyword Search (Ctrl+F / Cmd+F): Type any word into the Find in PDF... box in the floating toolbar and press Enter (or Shift+Enter for previous) to jump across matching pages and highlight matching text spans.

2.3 Entering Study Details, Auto-Fill & Quality Appraisal

Click the study header at the top of the right sidebar (✏️ Edit Study Info) to open the study information drawer:

  • ✨ Auto-fill (Page 1): Click the purple ✨ Auto-fill (Page 1) button at the top of the drawer to automatically detect the First Author, Publication Year, and Full Title using the PDF's internal metadata and Page 1 layout heuristics.
  • Quick Fill from Selection: While the Edit Study Info drawer is open, select any text in the PDF and click the quick buttons (➔ Author, ➔ Year, ➔ Title, ➔ Setting, ➔ Sample, ➔ Method) in the popup menu—or drag selected text directly into the input boxes.
  • Quality Appraisal & Conceptual Depth: Choose your appraisal tool (CASP, JBI, MMAT, ETQS, Dixon-Woods, or Custom), select an overall quality rating (High / Moderate / Low / Not appraised), and rate the paper's Conceptual Depth (Richness) as Rich (Detailed), Moderate, or Thin (Basic / Descriptive).

2.4 Extracting 1st Order Quotes & 2nd Order Concepts

Select any passage in the PDF with your mouse (multi-page selections across page breaks, such as p.2-3, are supported automatically). A dark popup bar will appear:

🟦 1st Order (Shortcut: Press 1)

Use this for direct participant quotes (e.g., italicized interview excerpts in the Results section).

🟩 2nd Order (Shortcut: Press 2)

Use this for the primary authors' interpretations, themes, and concepts.

  • Manual Entry for Scanned / Non-OCR PDFs: If you are working with an older scanned PDF where text cannot be highlighted, use the Manual Entry (No-OCR) bar above the construct list (`+ 🟦 1st Order` / `+ 🟩 2nd Order`) to type or paste quotes and page numbers manually.
  • Switching Between 1st and 2nd Order: Tagged a quote as 1st Order when it should be 2nd Order (or vice versa)? Click the ⇄ To 2nd or ⇄ To 1st button on the card header to convert it in one click.
  • Linking Quotes to Concepts: Use the 🔗 Link to 2nd Order... dropdown on a blue quote card, or drag the blue quote card and drop it directly onto a green 2nd Order card.
  • Fixing Two-Column PDF Line Breaks: Click ✏️ Edit Quote (or double-click the quote text) to clean up hyphenations, line breaks, or page headers.
  • Private Reading Notes: Each card has a collapsible 🌐 Reading & Nuance Notes (Private) box for personal reading memos or translation nuances—these auto-save quietly without cluttering your formal Audit Trail.
Section 3

Tab 2: Synthesis Matrix, Line of Argument & CERQual

Tab 2 is where you translate concepts across studies to build your 3rd Order Themes. Studies appear as columns across the top (starting with your Index Paper), and your 3rd Order Themes appear as rows down the left.

3.1 Creating 3rd Order Theme Rows & Mapping Concepts

  • Adding a Theme Row: Click + Add Theme Row (3rd Order) at either the top-right toolbar or the bottom of the matrix table. You can also drag a 2nd Order card from the left pool onto the dashed drop zone to create a new row automatically.
  • Placing Concepts into the Matrix: Drag any 2nd Order concept from the left pool and drop it into a theme row. It will automatically snap into the column for its study! A single 2nd Order concept can be placed into multiple rows if it relates to more than one theme.
  • Using Unlinked 1st Order Quotes: If a primary study is mostly descriptive and has a rich quote without a clear author concept, click + Convert to 2nd Order (or drag the quote directly into a matrix row) to wrap it into a concept card.
  • Reordering Theme Rows: Use the ▲ and ▼ buttons on any 3rd Order row header to organize the sequence of your themes.
  • Searching & Filtering the Left Concept Pool: Use the search box, study dropdown filter, and ⚡ Refut. checkbox at the top of the left pool to quickly locate specific 2nd Order concepts across dozens of studies.

3.2 Marking Similarities (Reciprocal) vs. Contradictions (Refutational)

A key part of meta-ethnography is looking not only at how studies agree, but also at where they differ or contradict one another:

  • Inside Each Cell: Every concept card placed in the matrix has a toggle button at the top-left: 🤝 Reciprocal. Click it to switch the card to ⚡ Refutational (the card turns rose-red) and add a note explaining why this study's finding contrasts with the others in that row.
  • Filter for Contradictions: Check the ⚡ Contradictions Only toggle in the top toolbar to filter the matrix to rows containing refutational findings.

3.3 Jumping Back to the Exact PDF Page

You never lose sight of the original context in the matrix. Every card in the left pool and inside the matrix cells features a prominent button: PDF p.4.

Clicking this button immediately switches back to Tab 1, opens that study's PDF to the exact page, scrolls to the highlighted passage, and flashes it in yellow so you can re-read the surrounding paragraph. Linked 1st Order quotes inside matrix cells also have their own clickable p.X buttons.

3.4 Using Compact View, GRADE-CERQual & Line of Argument

  • 🔭 Compact View: When you have many studies and themes, check 🔭 Compact View in the top toolbar to hide full quotes and comment boxes so you can see your entire matrix at a glance.
  • 📊 CERQual Tab (Inside Each Row Header): Click the 📊 CERQual mini-tab on the left side of any row to assess Methodological Limitations, Coherence, Adequacy, and Relevance (every domain starts as Not assessed). Click ✨ Auto-suggest to generate a suggested confidence rating (High, Moderate, Low, Very low) and draft an explanation based on your ratings and contributing studies.
  • 🔗 Research Aim & Overall Model Drawer: Click the purple 🔗 Research Aim & Overall Model button in the top bar to open a drawer where you can keep your Phase 1 research question visible and write your overarching Line of Argument model. Click your 3rd Order theme chips (`+ [Theme Name]`) and arrow buttons (`➔`, `⇄`, `⚡`) to build a text diagram showing how your themes connect. Under Concept relationship map, add relations between themes to draw a circular or left-to-right map, and use Download .svg to save it.
Section 4

Tab 3: Audit Trail & Reflexive Log

The eMERGe guideline asks researchers to explain how interpretive decisions were made and how the researchers' own backgrounds influenced the synthesis (reflexivity).

  • Automatic Logging of Key Decisions: Whenever you create or rename a 3rd Order theme, change a study's order/Index Paper, mark a construct as Refutational, update CERQual ratings, or save your Line of Argument model, the app automatically logs a timestamped entry showing the Before ➔ After change.
  • Adding Reflexive & Analytic Memos: Use the form on the left side of Tab 3 to record your personal clinical or academic lens (Reflexivity), notes from supervisor/team meetings, or reasons for grouping themes in a certain way.
  • Adding Reasons to Any Log Entry: Every item in the chronological timeline has a + Add reason for this decision... box where you can type a quick rationale.
  • Filtering & Controlling Log Detail: Use the category dropdown in the top-right corner to filter logs by phase (e.g., 3rd Order Evolution, GRADE-CERQual, Team Concordance). By default, routine drag-and-drop actions are hidden so your log stays focused on interpretive decisions; check Also log minor drag-and-drop actions if you want every quote link and card placement recorded.
Section 5

Tab 4: Export Tables for Your Manuscript

Tab 4 first compiles an eMERGe Methods Summary (with a 19-item checklist mapping) from your project data, then formats your work into five publication-ready academic tables for your dissertation, journal manuscript, or supplementary appendix:

  1. Table 1 — Study Characteristics & Appraisal: Lists all included studies in your chosen translation order (#1 Index Paper first), along with setting, sample, methods, quality appraisal ratings, and conceptual depth.
  2. Table 2 — Synthesis & Line of Argument: Shows each 3rd Order Theme, its translation type, your synthesis note, supporting 2nd Order concepts, illustrating 1st Order quotes (with page numbers), contributing studies, and CERQual confidence.
  3. Table 2b — Concept × Study Contribution Grid: A compact cross-tabulation matrix showing which primary studies contributed to each 3rd Order theme (● = Reciprocal contribution; ⚡ = Refutational contribution; — = Not present), along with page numbers and study counts (n/N).
  4. Table 3 — GRADE-CERQual SoQF Table: A Summary of Qualitative Findings (SoQF) and/or Evidence Profile showing all four CERQual domains and your explanation for each finding. Choose a combined table, SoQF only, Evidence Profile only, or two separate tables (3a / 3b) from the dropdown above the preview.
  5. Appendix — Audit Trail: A chronological table of your interpretive decisions, team concordance records, and reflexive memos.

Export Options: Click Copy Rich Table to paste the currently viewed table directly into Microsoft Word or Google Docs with borders and formatting intact. You can also Copy Markdown, download the Methods Summary and all five tables together in a single landscape Word (.docx) document, or download a Raw CSV of your entire synthesis matrix.

Section 6

Comparing Reviewer Files & Keyboard Shortcuts

6.1 Asynchronous Team Workflow & Comparison (Compare Button)

To respect publisher copyright and keep backup files lightweight, PDF files themselves are not saved inside .json backups. Because the app reconnects PDFs by matching their filenames, we strongly recommend the following workflow when working with co-reviewers across institutions:

Recommended Step-by-Step Team Setup
  1. Prepare a Private Shared Folder: Place all included full-text PDFs in a private, access-restricted shared folder (e.g., institutional OneDrive, Google Drive, Dropbox, or a shared Zotero group) so every reviewer uses the exact same PDF filenames.
  2. Lead Reviewer Creates the "Starter JSON": One lead reviewer sets the Project Code and Phase 1 Aim, loads all PDFs from the shared folder, fills in the basic citation details (using ✨ Auto-fill), and clicks Backup to export a starter .json template.
  3. Co-Reviewers Restore & Reconnect PDFs: Co-reviewers open the app, set their own Reviewer ID, and load the starter .json via Settings (⚙️) ➔ Restore Project. They then drag and drop the PDFs from the shared folder into the window—the app will automatically link every PDF to its study entry by filename.
  4. Extract Independently, Then Compare & Merge: After each reviewer extracts quotes and concepts on their own computer, have your co-reviewer click Backup and send you their .json file. Click the teal Compare button in the top bar and drop their file in to view overlap rates, Import & Agree, Import for Discussion, or Interleave Audit Logs in one click.
